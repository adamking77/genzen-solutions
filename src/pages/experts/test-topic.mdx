## Situation Assessment

Shadow networks usually surface after a control break: a client changes legal counsel, leaves a restrictive community, or confronts financial exploitation. We start with friction mapping—pinpointing where autonomy is being suppressed or narrative control is enforced—and document every operator, channel, and dependency keeping the structure intact.

## Evidence Stack

1. **Narrative capture:** Identify phrases, documents, and rituals repeating across actors. These shared scripts reveal command channels faster than device forensics.
2. **Resource choke points:** Follow the money, transportation, and document custody. Operators cannot maintain leverage without a material choke point.
3. **Psychological anchors:** Catalog the promises and fears being weaponized. We neutralize leverage more quickly when we know which myths and threats are in circulation.

## Intervention Blueprint

### 1. Counter-brief the target
Deliver a concise threat memo to the person under pressure. We foreground facts, provide scripts for disengagement, and design a safe escalation path if retaliation occurs.

### 2. Expose the network asymmetrically
Release a calibrated evidence packet—private arbitration letter, board briefing, or legal hold—that forces operators into the open without giving them a counter-narrative to weaponize.

### 3. Remap trust and leverage
Insert trusted stewards into financial, legal, and communications flows. At the same time, we sunset legacy intermediaries so the network cannot reconstitute through soft channels.

## Outcomes To Track

- **Signal strength:** The number of unsolicited contacts and intimidation attempts should drop within 72 hours.
- **Autonomy markers:** Clients report new decision windows—being able to schedule, travel, or communicate without interference.
- **Paper trail control:** Every critical document is mirrored and monitored; tampering attempts are logged and relayed to counsel automatically.
